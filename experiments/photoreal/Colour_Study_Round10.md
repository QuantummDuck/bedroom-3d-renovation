# Round 10: vertical slat wall and colour study

**Final file:** `experiments/teak_navy/Bedroom_v11_Smoked_Teal.blend`
**Slats only, original colours:** `Bedroom_Teak_Navy_v10_slats.blend`

## 1. The slat wall

The horizontal boards on the bed/shelf wall are replaced by vertical slats proportioned like real acoustic slat panels. The common retail spec is 27 mm slats, 12 mm deep, with 13 mm gaps on felt.

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Slat width | 2.7 cm |
| Slat depth (stands proud of the backing) | 1.2 cm |
| Gap between slats | 1.35 cm. I stretched the 1.3 cm spec slightly so a full slat lands in each corner, with no cut slat. |
| Pitch | 4.05 cm, 72 slats across the 290 cm wall |
| Backing | 0.9 cm felt, visible in the gaps |
| Height | floor to the crown molding, with a 5 mm shadow gap at the floor |
| Slat face | in the same plane as the old boards, so the shelves, table, bed, plug and drawer unit didn't move |

**Why these proportions:**
- About one third of the surface is gap. That gives the fine pin-stripe rhythm in your reference photos without looking busy.
- The 12 mm depth throws a real shadow line in grazing light, under the shelf LEDs and the low sun.
- Each slat keeps a slightly different wood tone, as real veneer does.

## 2. How I chose the colours

**Fixed elements.** These can't be repainted, so every palette had to work with them:
- the greige porcelain floor
- the white window frame
- your **blue MX6 chair**
- the grey/mustard/white bedspread
- the black steel table legs and shelf frames

**The constant:** the wood, which covers the slat wall, desk, bed panel, wardrobe strips, shelves and drawer unit.

**Wood first.** The scanned teak read too orange next to every paint colour.
- I graded it to a deeper, less saturated oiled teak, `#86603C`, close to the walnut-teak in your references.
- It's one wood across the whole room, so the shelves now match the slats.
- The improvement was clear in every palette.

**Principles applied:**
- **Colour drenching.** Walls, skirting/crown, door, door frame and the wardrobe all take one colour.
  - In a small room, contrasting trim draws every edge and corner and makes the box obvious.
  - Drenching blurs the edges, so the room reads as one continuous volume, and the 181 cm wardrobe recedes into the wall instead of standing in front of it.
  - Designers use this to make small dark rooms feel cocooning rather than cramped.
- **Complementary versus analogous.** The wood is orange-brown, roughly 30° on the colour wheel.
  - Its complement, blue-green, gives the richest contrast.
  - Neighbouring hues (clay, olive) give the calmest harmony.
  - The finalists cover both approaches.
- **Muted, not saturated.** Each wall colour is greyed.
  - Saturated colour at this scale makes a small room feel louder and smaller.
  - A greyed colour lets the wood and the lights be the warm, saturated elements.
- **Light reflectance value (LRV) and spaciousness:**
  - The dark options are drenched with a warm tinted ceiling (LRV ~61), not stark white, so the room keeps its height without a bright "lid".
  - The vertical slat lines add perceived height.
- **One undertone family.** Every wall colour has a warm or grey undertone that agrees with the greige floor and the wood. Nothing is icy.

## 4. The four finalists

| | Walls, trim, door, wardrobe | Ceiling | Slat gaps | Approx. LRV | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Smoked Teal** (recommended) | `#36433F` | `#D4CCBD` | `#141716` | ~5 | soft complementary: cocooning, calm, ties the wood to your blue chair |
| Olive Library | `#4B4A37` | `#D6CDB8` | `#1A1A13` | ~7 | analogous and earthy, like an old library; the most "natural" |
| Clay Cocoon | `#A48B76` | `#D8CBB9` | `#2A1F18` | ~28 | warm tonal mid-tone; the most spacious and brightest of the cozy options |
| Ink & Deep Teak | `#283240` | `#D3CABB` | `#0F0F10` | ~3 | high-contrast complementary; the most dramatic, close to your current navy |

In all four, the wardrobe strips are wood and everything wood is deep teak.

## 5. Why Smoked Teal

**What it is.** `#36433F` is a blue-green, the complement of the teak's orange-brown, mixed so grey it doesn't read as either green or blue.

**Why it wins:**
- **Contrast.** It gives the wood the strongest glow of the four. Because it's so muted, the contrast stays soft, which is what reads as cozy rather than dramatic.
- **The chair.** It is the only palette where your blue chair belongs to the scheme, as a brighter note of the wall colour, instead of fighting it (Olive) or disappearing into it (Ink).
- **Light.** By day it reads as a soft grey-green, never dark. In the golden-hour sun it turns warm brown-grey. At night it falls back and lets the slat wall and the shelf light carry the room.
- **Space.** Drenched with a warm tinted ceiling, the corners and the wardrobe dissolve, so the room feels like one deep, calm volume. Ink & Deep Teak goes cave-dark at night, and Clay is spacious but less enveloping.

**What it changes, physically:**
- **Paint:** walls 1, 2 and 4, the skirting, crown molding and cove ledge, the door and its frame, and the wardrobe body and doors, all in `#36433F`.
  - Use **matt** on the walls and **satin** on the wood or MDF parts (skirting, door, wardrobe), so the joinery wipes clean.
- **Ceiling:** warm off-white `#D4CCBD`.
- **Slat gaps:** near-black `#141716`.
- **Wood:** deeper oiled teak on everything wood.

## 6. Notes

- **Paint codes:** the hex codes are on-screen colours. Before buying, match them at a paint store to a real paint with a large sample on your wall. Your west light will warm them.
- **Night renders:** they are dominated by the blue bed-panel LED you chose. It suits Smoked Teal and Ink, but clashes with Clay.
- **Switching palettes:** `palettes.py` applies any palette to any of these files:

```bash
"$B" -b ../teak_navy/Bedroom_Teak_Navy_v10_slats.blend --python palettes.py -- save=R2 path=OUT.blend
```
