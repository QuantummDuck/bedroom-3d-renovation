## The navy

All variations share one navy, `#283548`: deep blue with a grey-green undertone.
- **Why this navy:** a purer blue navy reads cold next to warm wood. A slight grey-green undertone sits closer to the orange-brown teak, so the pair feels warm rather than icy.
- **The wood:** deep oiled teak `#86603C` everywhere, carried over from round 10.

## How I built the variations

Navy is the foundation in each one. What changes is:
- the **second colour** (camel, oat, brass, brick, stone)
- **where navy goes**: everywhere (drench), on the walls only, or on the built joinery only
- the **wood tone**

**Principles behind them:**
- **Complementary pairing.** Navy is the complement of orange-brown wood and of warm neutrals, so each pairs richly with navy.
- **60/30/10 proportions.** Roughly: navy on the walls (60%), wood and the second colour (30%), and your chair and bedspread as the accents (10%).
- **Light and dark balance.** A small room needs one large lighter surface to avoid feeling closed in. The biggest candidate is the 181 × 270 cm wardrobe.
- **One temperature family.** Every second colour is warm and muted, so nothing competes with the wood.

### Why it wins

- **Navy stays the envelope.** Walls, trim and door are drenched, so the corners blur and the room reads as one deep, enclosing space. That is the cocoon you get from navy.
- **The wardrobe is the one large warm, lighter surface.** It's the biggest object in the room. In camel instead of navy it:
  - bounces daylight and lamplight back into the room
  - stops wall 4 from closing in
  - sits half-way between the navy and the teak in value, so the contrast stays soft rather than stark, unlike cream (M3)
- **A clear colour structure:**
  - Navy and camel are complementary, so the pairing is rich.
  - Camel and teak are neighbours, so the pairing is harmonious.
  - Navy sits once on the wardrobe, in the inlay strips, tying the two together.
- **It works at all three times of day:**
  - By day, the room reads navy and camel.
  - At golden hour, the camel glows like leather in the low sun.
  - At night, the navy steps back and the slat wall, shelf light and camel carry the room.
- **Your chair and bedspread fit:** the chair's blue belongs to the navy family, and the bedspread's grey and mustard sit inside the warm-neutral range.

## Practical notes

- **Paint codes:** the colours are on-screen values. Match them at a paint store with large samples on your walls, viewed in your afternoon west light, which warms them.
- **Real paints close to this direction** (for reference only, not checked against the renders):
  - deep navies with a green-grey undertone, such as Farrow & Ball Hague Blue or Stiffkey Blue
  - camel and leather-tan joinery colours
- **Wardrobe finish:** satin lacquer, so the camel doors wipe clean and catch a soft sheen.
- **Night lighting:** the blue bed-panel LED fits naturally with navy. If you ever want the night look warmer, set that strip to 2700 K.
